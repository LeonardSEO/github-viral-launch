# From repository to a shareable launch

Read for launch preparation and diagnosis. Scale the effort to the repository and maintainer's available time.

## Give developers a reason to share

Find the moment someone would send to a colleague: a tedious task visibly finished, a surprising capability, a clear explanation, or a useful result with less setup. State audience, pain, demonstrated result, and honest distinction in one sentence. Choose one angle supported by actual code/examples.

Show the result in a real 5–10 second recording: exact operation, input, visible output, and README placement. Keep text legible without sound and include accessible accompanying text. If recording is unavailable, deliver a shot list and suitable command; say media still needs recording. Do not pass mockups off as working software.

Keep the README opening compact: one-liner, visual, install, smallest useful example, and trial/docs link. Adapt ordering to the project; do not force an eight-line layout that makes examples unreadable. Keep prerequisites visible. A screenshot/video/diagram may be a justified alternative; report its difference from item 1.

Make the route from demo to useful trial obvious. Offer a reusable example, shareable output, or focused comparison when supported by the project. Explain why a developer would share it. Product changes beyond launch preparation need their own scope. Avoid forced referrals and surprise telemetry.

## Prepare the launch package

Reuse existing material and fill real gaps:

- README opening and verified first useful example.
- Real demo or explicitly labeled recording plan; social preview asset or brief.
- Relevant description/topics and release-note draft.
- Justified contributor issue drafts and response coverage.
- Show HN author facts brief when eligible, without generated submission copy.
- Distinct channel-specific draft copy only where current rules allow it.

Ground claims in code, reproduction steps, or observable examples. State maturity, limitations, prerequisites, and commercial boundaries. Do not invent user counts, endorsements, integrations, or performance improvements. Explain why the maintainer built it and what they learned.

## Choose channels and sequence attention

Pick one primary discovery channel based on audience fit, demonstration fit, community requirements, and reply capacity. Show HN is a candidate, not an automatic destination. For a small launch add at most two relevant secondary communities; expand only when fit and capacity justify it. Verify current rules and self-promotion constraints. Do not invent activity, account history, or moderator approval.

For each channel specify reader, reason the repo belongs, useful angle, allowed asset, destination URL, owner, posting window/timezone, and reply coverage. Stagger posts so the maintainer can participate. Choose timing around audience and availability; universal best hours and star-velocity recipes are hypotheses, not facts.

Do not mass-mention maintainers, cross-post identical promotional text, solicit HN votes/comments/submissions, or delete/repost to reset attention. Draft approval alone does not authorize publication; require authorization covering actual account, channel, content, and timing. After an ambiguous write result inspect the destination before retrying to prevent duplicates.

If HN is unsuitable, retain item 15 with N/A and explain the alternative. When rules/access cannot be checked, label the action unverified and finish other preparation.

## Run a launch the maintainer can sustain

- **Before publicity:** resolve actual blockers, verify the trial path, prepare assets/notes, check channel eligibility, and capture available baselines.
- **Launch window:** execute only authorized publication, keep trial links prominent, and reserve substantive reply time. Do not invent the maintainer's commitment.
- **Next 24–72 hours:** triage, fix reproducible first-run problems, credit contributors, and update docs from actual questions. Keep sensitive reports private via the project's contact route.
- **Day 7 and day 30:** examine trial/continued use and adjust the next experiment. Share another update only when there is new substance.

For item 16 name an owner, timezone, two daily triage windows, backup/unavailable-period handling, and a 24-hour first-response target. If capacity cannot cover this, show the gap and offer a smaller launch or honest expectation. Do not create monitoring jobs or reminders without a user request.

## Measure and diagnose

Capture baselines with dates, revision, and source: stars, visitors/referrers, trial activity, downloads, outside contributions. Do not add analytics infrastructure merely to fill a table. Traffic may require elevated access and have short retention; say what is missing. Downloads/clones include possible bots and CI, not necessarily unique users.

Use day 1 for reach/sharing, day 7 for trial, and day 30 for return use/contribution. These are review windows, not virality standards. For a star-growth goal report net stars alongside at least one available trial/contribution signal. Base targets on real baselines and capacity; use explicitly provisional absolute targets when the baseline is zero. Do not assert a viral coefficient without referral/adoption data.

| Evidence | Smallest next experiment |
|---|---|
| Little qualified reach | Better channel fit or a clearer demonstrated result. |
| Visits without attempts | Improve clarity, prerequisites, demo, or first-run friction. |
| Failed attempts | Fix the observed install, permissions, dependency, or example failure first. |
| Stars without trial signals | Check promise versus first experience and acknowledge measurement gaps. |
| Trial without return | Investigate continuing value/reliability before more promotion. |
| Useful issues but few PRs | Improve issue scope, setup, and review responsiveness. |

Treat diagnoses as hypotheses. Missing telemetry does not prove missing use.
